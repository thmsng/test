How to read the report

Five stages, from green to bare

Park staff report two numbers: how much of the canopy has changed colour, and how many leaves have fallen. We group them into five stages so you can see at a glance whether it's worth the drive.

    Colour change

    The share of leaves that have turned from green. Most parks look their best from about 70% to 90%, before many leaves have dropped.

    Leaf fall

    The share of leaves already on the ground. Once it passes half, trails look thinner, but the forest floor is a carpet of colour.

    Main colour

    The colour you'll see most of. It comes from the trees in each park:

    Yellow: birch, poplar, aspen, tamarack Orange: sugar maple Red: red maple, sumac, red oak

    Toronto spots worth a walk

    Riverdale Park East Broadview Ave

    The hill that looks west over the Don Valley to the skyline. Maple Hill is modelled on it.

    High Park Bloor St W

    Rare black oak savanna, Grenadier Pond and long loops under maples and oaks.

    Evergreen Brick Works Bayview Ave

    Ravine trails and a quarry pond in the Don Valley, with a lookout over the treetops.

    Edwards Gardens Lawrence Ave E

    Formal gardens leading down into the wooded Wilket Creek ravine.

    Rouge National Urban Park Eastern edge of the city

    Canada's first national urban park, with forest, marsh and river valley trails.
